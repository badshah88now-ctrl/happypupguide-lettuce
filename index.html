Can Dogs Eat Lettuce? Plain Leaves Are Fine in Small Amounts

By HappyPupGuide

Yes — plain lettuce is safe for most dogs. Lettuce is mostly water, very low in calories, and easy to chew, so a few plain leaves make a light, crunchy treat. It suits dogs that need to watch their weight, because it adds bulk and crunch without adding much food energy. The rule that matters is simple: plain leaves only, with no dressing, salt, or toppings.

Both common types are fine. Romaine lettuce is the better choice, because it holds a little more nutrition than iceberg, but iceberg is also safe — it is simply mostly water with very little else in it. Either way, wash the leaves first and tear them into small pieces that suit the size of the dog. Big whole leaves can be awkward to chew and swallow, so small pieces are the safe way to offer them.

Keep the portion small. A few torn leaves are enough, and a large pile is not a good idea — too much lettuce at once can loosen the stools, because of the water and fibre. Lettuce should never be served as a salad: dressings, oils, onions, garlic, salt and cheese are all common salad additions that are not suitable for dogs. Plain leaves straight from the bag, washed and torn up, are the only kind to offer.

Lettuce is a treat, not a meal. Treats of any kind should stay a small part of the day's food, with the dog's regular balanced diet doing the main work. Dogs with sensitive stomachs should only try new foods after a vet's okay, and any new food can cause soft stools or gas the first time. If you notice vomiting or diarrhoea, stop and go back to plain food for a day, and speak to your vet if it does not settle.
